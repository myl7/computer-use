\begin{abstract}
Computer-use agents can save tokens by compiling shared GUI procedures into programs.
However, compilation can fail, its cost is uncertain, and future tasks may not repeat enough to recover that cost.
GUI changes can also make programs unusable.
We introduce PACE (Price-Aware Compilation from Experience), which combines a measurement protocol with an online compilation rule.
The protocol measures compilation costs, including failed attempts, and compares agent and program costs on matched task inputs.
The online rule uses past arrivals and compilation outcomes to propose compilation when projected savings justify its cost.
Under stated action-cost assumptions, separate budget checks keep total cost within $1+\epsilon$ times an agent-only reference after each arrival.
Across seven task families and three models, verified programs have estimated per-use savings of $79.4\%$ to nearly $100\%$.
In simulations across twelve model--log conditions, PACE averages $0.781$ of agent-only cost with $\epsilon=0.25$, compared with $1.062$ and $0.995$ for AutoRPA and ToolPro decision-rule adaptations.
Ablations show that the compilation proposal reduces average cost, while the budget limits large cost increases.
\end{abstract}
